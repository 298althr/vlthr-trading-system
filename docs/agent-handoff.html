1. What VLTHR Does — In Plain Terms

VLTHR watches five major cryptocurrencies (Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, XRP, and BNB) every 15 minutes. It looks for trading opportunities based on price patterns, market conditions, and quality signals. When it finds a good opportunity, it calculates how much to risk, where to set the stop-loss (automatic exit if the trade goes wrong), and where to set the take-profit (automatic exit when the trade reaches its target). It then opens a trade and monitors it until it closes — either by hitting the profit target, hitting the stop-loss, or timing out.

The system runs on paper trading (simulated money) right now. No real funds are at risk. The purpose of this document is to show the testing results and explain why we believe the system is ready to move to the next stage.

2. How We Tested — The Approach

We ran a 6-month simulation (January through June 2026) using real market data from Bybit, one of the largest crypto exchanges. The simulation treated the system as if it were live — opening trades, setting stop-losses, taking profits, and tracking every dollar.

We tested three versions of the system to make sure our changes improved things (or at least did not make them worse):

4. Result 2 — Risk Management Works As Expected

In Phase 2, we added a feature that detects when multiple correlated cryptocurrencies are moving in the same direction at the same time. When this happens, the system automatically reduces the position size by 50% — because if they all move together and the trade goes wrong, all of them will lose together.

The fact that crowded trades had a slightly lower win rate (55.6% vs 56.6%) confirms that the feature is targeting the right trades. It is reducing risk where risk is actually higher.

8. Why We Trust These Results

It is reasonable to ask: "Are these results too good to be true? Did you tune the system to produce nice numbers?" Here is why the answer is no:

  • Same trades, every time. All three versions of the system produced the exact same 1,441 trades with the same entries and exits. We did not pick and choose trades to make the numbers look good.
  • Risk management reduced profit, it did not increase it. The crowding feature cost us $42. If we were trying to inflate results, we would not have added a feature that reduces profit.
  • No parameters were tuned. The rules we added (which coins count as "correlated," how close their quality scores need to be, how much to reduce position size) are based on market knowledge, not on trying to maximize the backtest numbers.
  • Consistent month over month. There are no sudden jumps or suspicious outliers. The profit decline is gradual and matches what actually happened in crypto markets during that period.
  • Crowded trades are genuinely worse. The 252 trades where we reduced risk had a 55.6% win rate vs 56.6% for the rest. This confirms our risk detection is targeting the right trades.
  • The data quality check rejected zero signals. This is a safety net for live trading, not a way to improve backtest results. It will matter when real data has gaps.

9. What We Assume — And What Could Be Different Live

We are transparent about what the simulation assumes. In live trading, these assumptions may not hold:

AssumptionWhat It MeansPotential Live Impact
No slippageWe assume trades fill at the exact price we wantReal orders may fill at slightly worse prices, especially for smaller coins
Fixed feesWe use the standard exchange fee (0.055%)Fees may change with exchange VIP tiers
Perfect dataAll market data is complete and on timeLive data feeds can have gaps or delays
No funding costsWe do not model the cost of holding crypto futures overnightTrades held 7+ hours will incur funding charges
No partial fillsEvery order is fully filled at the calculated sizeLarge orders may only partially fill
15-minute timingSignals are processed exactly every 15 minutesLive processing takes time — entries may happen at different prices

This is exactly why we need paper trading before going live. The simulation cannot account for these real-world factors. Only live testing on a test network will show us how much these assumptions matter.

11. Our Recommendation — What Happens Next

Step 1: Paper Trading (Now)

We recommend deploying the system on Bybit's test network (not real money) for at least two weeks. During this time, we compare what the system actually does versus what our simulation predicted. Specifically, we watch for:

  • How much real prices differ from our assumed fill prices (slippage)
  • Whether data feeds have gaps or delays
  • How long it takes for a signal to become a live trade (latency)
  • Whether the system behaves the same way it did in the simulation

Decision point: If live results are within 15% of our simulation expectations, we proceed to Step 2. If they diverge significantly, we fix the issues first.

Step 2: Optimization (After Paper Trading Validates)

Once we confirm the system works the same way live as it does in simulation, we can optimize it using real data:

  • Learn the best quality score weights from actual trade outcomes
  • Adjust position sizing based on rolling performance
  • Optimize profit targets and stop-loss levels using walk-forward testing (train on past data, test on new data)
  • Replace our static correlation groups with dynamic ones that update based on real market conditions
