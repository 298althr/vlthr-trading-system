1. The Problem We Found

The original VLTHR system had all its decision-making bundled into one large process. The part that finds trading opportunities, the part that decides how much to risk, and the part that records trades were all tangled together. This created three problems:

1

Finding vs Deciding Were Mixed

The opportunity finder was also making risk decisions. A signal that should have been passed to the risk team for evaluation was being blocked at the source — before anyone could assess whether it was worth taking with a smaller position size.

2

One Failure Rolled Back Everything

Because all parts ran in a single transaction, if one step failed (say, writing to the database), it would undo all the work from every other step — even the parts that succeeded. This made the system fragile.

3

No Separation of Concerns

The opportunity finder knew about existing open positions. It should not. Finding opportunities and managing open positions are different jobs. Mixing them made the code hard to test and hard to trust.

2. What We Changed — In Simple Terms

We separated the system into clear roles. Think of it like a trading desk: one person finds opportunities, another decides how much to risk, another places the orders, and another watches the positions. Each person does one job and hands off to the next.

📊

Market Data (The Eyes)

Pulls live prices from Bybit every 60 seconds. This is the only part that touches raw market data. Everyone else gets their data from here.

🧠

Signal Finder (The Brain)

Scans the market every 15 minutes and identifies trading opportunities. It scores each opportunity based on technical patterns, market structure, and context. It does not know about open positions. It does not decide how much to risk. It just finds and ranks.

🛡️

Risk Manager (The Gatekeeper)

Takes the ranked opportunities and decides: Should we trade this? If yes, how much should we risk? Where should we set the stop-loss? Are we already too exposed to this coin? Are too many correlated coins moving together? This is where position sizing happens.

⚡

Execution (The Hands)

Takes approved trades and places them. Handles the transition from "pending" to "open." If the entry price has moved, it recalculates whether the trade is still worth taking.

📒

Record Keeper (The Books)

Maintains the trade ledger, account balance, and risk exposure. Every trade is recorded with full details. This is the source of truth for all financial reporting.

🔭

Trade Monitor (The Watchdog)

Checks every 15 seconds whether any open trade has hit its profit target, stop-loss, or time limit. Closes trades automatically when needed.

Each part does its job and hands off to the next. If one part fails, the others can still function. If the signal finder goes down, the trade monitor keeps watching open positions. If the trade monitor goes down, the signal finder keeps finding opportunities. This is the key benefit of separation.

4. What We Added in Phase 2

Implemented
🛡️

Crowding Detection

When Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana all signal "buy" at the same time with similar quality scores, they are likely moving together. If the trade goes wrong, all three will lose together. The system now detects this and automatically cuts the position size in half for these trades.

Result: 252 crowded trades identified. Win rate on these: 55.6% vs 56.6% for non-crowded — confirming they are slightly riskier.

Implemented
📋

Data Quality Checks

Before acting on any signal, the system checks: Is the market data fresh? Is it complete? Are there any unusual price spikes that might be errors? If the data quality score is below 60, the signal is rejected.

Result: Zero rejections in testing (data was clean). This is a safety net for live trading where data gaps can happen.

Implemented
📝

Full Decision Tracking

Every signal the system sees is now logged with: what the system intended to do (trade, reject, or veto), what actually happened (win, loss, expired, or never traded), whether it was crowded, and its data quality score. This creates a complete audit trail.

Result: 64,179 rows of decision data across 3 system versions. Every signal is traceable from birth to death.
