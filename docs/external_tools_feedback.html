CITM: Comprehensive Intelligence Twin Model

What Is CITM?

CITM is a domain-independent Intelligence Operating System. It models reality before modelling solutions. Every solution has an objective, a problem, constraints, causes, evidence, uncertainty, consequences, execution requirements, and scientific value. The architecture transforms reality into executable knowledge through a continuous intelligence pipeline. All domains (trading, medicine, manufacturing, energy, etc.) plug into the same architecture through Domain Adapters. The core never changes.

  • Law 1: Every solution has a discoverable problem.
  • Law 2: Every problem originates from an objective.
  • Law 3: Every decision is constrained by reality.

    Design Principles

  • Every engine shall have one responsibility.
  • No duplicated intelligence, state, or evidence.
  • Every engine produces reusable, machine-readable outputs.
  • Every resource is versioned. Every relationship is explicit.
  • Every uncertainty is measurable. Every decision is reproducible.
  • Engines never communicate directly. Communication occurs through canonical resources (loose coupling).
  • Domains may add entities, constraints, evidence, simulations, execution models. Domains shall never modify Kernel, Runtime, Governance, Scientific promotion, Canonical ontology, Confidence model, or Traceability model.

    • PURF: Progressive Uncertainty Reduction Framework

    What Is PURF?

    PURF is not a root-cause analysis framework. It is a progressive uncertainty reduction framework. Every higher layer is only allowed to exist if the lower layer has passed validation. The pyramid is a sequence of gates rather than a sequence of analyses. The pyramid is fractal: each layer becomes a new pyramid. CITM sits between Reality and Reasoning, giving AI a structured representation of reality instead of text to reason over.

    Governance Layers 11 horizontal governance layers

    17-Level PURF Pyramid

    L0: Reality Capture - Capture challenge exactly as received. Gate: Complete capture.
    L1: Context Matrix - Stakeholders, objectives, constraints, environment. Gate: Context completeness.
    L2: Uncertainty Matrix - Facts, assumptions, opinions, hypotheses, missing info. Gate: Confidence score.
    L3: MECE Decomposition - Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive domains. Gate: Coverage test.
    L4: Digital Twin Construction - Mathematical/graph representation. Gate: Structural validity.
    L5: State Estimation - Current state of every subsystem. Gate: State consistency.
    L6: Root Cause Discovery - Trace causal chains, not symptoms. Gate: Evidence validation.
    L7: Knowledge Completion - Acquire missing data. Gate: Missing information threshold.
    L8: Hypothesis Generation - Competing explanations. Gate: Diversity test.
    L9: Hypothesis Elimination - Reject inconsistent explanations. Gate: Statistical confidence.
    L10: Option Generation - Possible interventions. Gate: Feasibility.
    L11: Simulation - Execute in digital twin. Gate: Performance metrics.
    L12: Trade-off Analysis - Cost, risk, safety, ethics, time. Gate: Pareto optimality.
    L13: Decision Approval - Choose preferred solution. Gate: Decision quality score.
    L14: Execution Planning - Tasks, dependencies, owners. Gate: Readiness.
    L15: Monitoring - Measure execution vs expectations. Gate: KPI health.
    L16: Learning - Compare prediction vs reality. Gate: Error analysis.
    L17: Memory - Store validated knowledge. Gate: Knowledge indexing.

    11 Horizontal Governance Layers

    AML: Assumption Management Layer

    Identifies, classifies, quantifies, validates, tracks, and monitors every assumption. No recommendation may rely on an undocumented assumption. Detects assumption drift. Triggers reassessment when assumptions become invalid.

    DEIL: Decision & Evidence Intelligence Layer

    Complete decision traceability from original challenge through every reasoning step to final outcome. No recommendation without an auditable evidence trail. Supports audits, regulatory reviews, post-incident investigations.

    UML: Uncertainty Management Layer

    Identifies unknowns, missing data, data quality issues, model limitations, operational/environmental/business uncertainty. Produces uncertainty register, confidence map, information gap report.

    VVL: Validation & Verification Layer

    Validation: "Did we build the correct solution?" Verification: "Did we build the solution correctly?" Governs every transition between PURF stages. Prevents propagation of invalid knowledge.

    AEL: Alternative Exploration Layer

    Prevents premature convergence. Enforces systematic exploration of competing explanations, architectures, technologies, and strategies. A solution cannot be approved unless it has competed against credible alternatives.

    RRL: Risk & Resilience Layer

    Risk identification, quantification, propagation, and resilience engineering across all PURF stages.

    VRL: Value & Resource Layer

    Commercial assessment, business value, CAPEX/OPEX, ROI, payback, revenue, savings, operational/safety/environmental benefits.

    XL: Execution Layer

    Execution planning, implementation, monitoring, and adaptation. Converts decisions into tasks, dependencies, owners, milestones.

    GCTL: Governance, Compliance & Trust Layer

    Enforces governance policies, validates regulatory compliance, ensures cybersecurity readiness, manages decision accountability, provides complete auditability. Every recommendation must satisfy governance before progressing.

    AOL: Action & Operations Layer

    Implementation planning, deployment, monitoring, and operational execution. Tasks, owners, timelines, budgets, milestones, rollback plans, KPIs.

    KEL: Knowledge Evolution Layer

    Transforms PURF from a decision framework into a continuously improving intelligence system. Every validated project permanently improves future reasoning, simulations, digital twins, and decisions. Makes CITM a learning operating system.

    • M.A.D. System: Sahara Group Competition Submission

    What Is the M.A.D. System?

    Machine Anomaly Detection. Addresses unplanned bearing failures at Asharami Energy and Egbin Power. Uses an LSTM autoencoder (62,293 parameters) trained on healthy-only vibration data to detect bearing degradation without fault labels. Delivers 51-hour lead time and 2.15% false alarm rate at under $500 per monitored bearing node. This is the filled, validated framework submission for a Sahara Group innovation challenge, using the CITM-PURF integrated workflow.

    4 Root Causes Identified (Five Whys)

  • Detection latency: Bearing degradation is gradual and temporal, but current methods are amplitude-based and non-temporal. Supervised ML cannot operate without fault labels.
  • False alarm burden: Operating regime variability is ignored in threshold setting, causing confusion between normal load changes and fault signatures.
  • Deployment infeasibility: Academic formulation optimizes for visualization quality, not real-time deployment. Deployable fixed-baseline alternative was not validated.
  • Validation rigour: Condition monitoring field has not adopted backtesting standards from quantitative finance.

    • 4 Solution Components

    1. LSTM Autoencoder

    62,293 params. 2-layer encoder (64->32), dense latent (16), 2-layer decoder (32->64). Sequence length 10. Trained on healthy data only. MAE anomaly score. AUC 0.9940 on NASA IMS.

    2. Regime Conditional DCE

    4-tier cascade: Normal/Watch/Alert/Critical. Per-regime mean offset + global std. 100-cycle persistence filter. 91.79% FAR reduction (26.41% to 2.15%).

    3. Deployable Health Index

    Fixed baseline (first 200 cycles). Z-score, exponential decay: HI = 100 * exp(-max(0, z_smooth) / 5.0). 0-100 scale. No retrospective data needed.

    4. Statistical Validation

    Walk-forward (T=200, W=100, S=50). Monte Carlo (1,000 permutations, p<0.001). 5-seed aggregation with 95% CI. 10 ablation studies.
